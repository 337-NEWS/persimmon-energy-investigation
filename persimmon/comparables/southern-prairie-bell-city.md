# Southern Prairie Solar & Storage — Bell City Comparative Project File

**Classification:** PRIMARY/SECONDARY SOURCE RESEARCH FILE  
**Purpose:** Comparison project for the Persimmon/nuclear investigation. Inclusion does not imply wrongdoing by the developer, landowner, public officials, cooperative, or supporting organizations.

## Project identity

- **Project:** Southern Prairie Solar & Storage
- **Location:** Bell City area, Calcasieu Parish; near Lionel Derouen and Fruge roads / Highway 27
- **Developer:** Southern Prairie Solar LLC / Westbridge Renewable Energy
- **Landowner/project partner reported locally:** Sweet Lake Land and Oil Company
- **Local proposal footprint:** approximately 1,100 acres
- **Local proposal output:** 200 MWac solar
- **Battery:** 55 MW in local approval reporting
- **Developer portfolio specification:** 300 MWdc solar + 110 MWh BESS
- **Interconnection:** local reporting says transmission will feed into the Jeff Davis Electric Cooperative grid

The 200 MWac/55 MW local figures and Westbridge's 300 MWdc/110 MWh specifications are not necessarily inconsistent: MWdc and MWac measure different sides of a solar facility, while MW battery power and MWh battery energy are different quantities. Preserve the units exactly.

## Calcasieu Parish approval

The Current reported that the Calcasieu Parish Planning and Zoning Board approved the proposal **8–1 on June 16, 2026**, with at-large member Genelle Hyatt casting the sole opposing vote.

Source:
https://thecurrentla.com/2026/calcasieu-solar-farm-sails-through-approval/

The report identifies:
- approximately 1,100 acres;
- Sweet Lake Land and Oil Company as the locally owned land company proposing the use;
- Southern Prairie Solar as a Westbridge subsidiary;
- 200 MWac generation;
- 55 MW battery storage;
- support from nearby residents, a major utility, Southwest Louisiana Economic Development Alliance and parish planning staff.

The Southwest Louisiana Economic Development Alliance reportedly characterized the Highway 27 corridor as a priority growth area for industrial and energy development consistent with the region's long-term economic strategy.

## Grid / cooperative connection

KPLC reported July 1, 2026 that the project's transmission lines would feed into the **Jeff Davis Electric Cooperative** power grid.

Source:
https://www.kplctv.com/2026/07/01/bell-city-solar-farm-approved-by-calcasieu-parish-planning-board/

Southern Prairie Vice President Margaret McKenna argued the project would improve reliability/resilience and reduce local marginal electricity pricing. These are developer claims and should be attributed as such.

## Particularly important comparison with Persimmon

Southern Prairie was approved only months before the Persimmon/Moss Bluff controversy and is within **Calcasieu Parish**, making it a stronger political control than an out-of-parish project.

The Current reports an 8–1 planning-board approval and substantial institutional support. Persimmon subsequently encountered organized opposition and intervention from regional legislators.

That difference is a reporting question, not evidence of inconsistency by itself. Potential explanations to test include:
- density of surrounding residences;
- existing land use;
- industrial-corridor designation;
- transmission access;
- project scale;
- environmental impacts;
- community support/opposition;
- setbacks and visual buffers;
- flood/drainage conditions;
- tax structure;
- ownership of the underlying land;
- proximity to existing industrial uses;
- battery component;
- possible relationship between the Persimmon site and later data-center development.
