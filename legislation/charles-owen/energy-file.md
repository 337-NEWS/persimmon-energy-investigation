## Legislation

- HR286 (2026): asks Louisiana Economic Development to study a "Louisiana Rural Community Energy and Education Investment Program."
- HR144 (2026): requests a peer-reviewed study of environmental impacts of captured-carbon streams.
- HB4 (2025): parish opt-in/opt-out authority over carbon sequestration wells (per Owen's own account).
- SB244 (2025, with Rep. Geymann): removed statutory language labeling greenhouse gases a threat to Louisiana; ensured captured carbon is treated like oil and gas in court for eminent-domain purposes.
- 2024: bill to remove electric vehicles from the state fleet (killed in committee, per Owen).
- 2021–2022: voted to end solar tax credits, restrict unchecked solar expansion, limit Chinese involvement in Louisiana energy projects (per Owen's own account).

## Public statements (energy)

- "Warning: Don't Trade the Farm for a Fad" (The Hayride, 9/28/2026): utility-scale solar framed as industrial development; calls for setbacks, notice, drainage rules, decommissioning bonds, and agricultural-land screens; argues solar cannot replace firm power; names gas and nuclear as dispatchable sources; blames weak timber markets for pushing landowners toward solar leases; notes discussions are occurring in western Louisiana.
- Multi-part carbon-capture series (The Hayride, 2025): consistent opposition to CCS eminent-domain expansion and 45Q-driven development.
- "Responding To A Very Bizarre Characterization" (The Hayride, 1/22/2026): rebuts characterization as an environmental radical; notes alliance with Reps. Geymann and Mike Johnson on property-rights energy fights.

## Contrast with Geymann (for the investigation)

Owen's identified energy-sector contributions are concentrated in traditional oil/gas and utilities, with no identified contributions in this review from solar developers, nuclear companies, LNG companies, or electric cooperatives. Geymann's reviewed record is more cross-cutting, including ENPAC, solar developers, LNG interests, and the cooperative sector.

The two legislators' public energy positions overlap in several areas, but campaign contributions alone do not establish influence, coordination, or causation. Any headline contribution should be verified against the underlying filed report before publication.
