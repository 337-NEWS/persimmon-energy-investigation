# Entergy 2Q 2026 earnings presentation — investigation index

**Source type:** PRIMARY SOURCE  
**Document:** Entergy 2Q 2026 earnings call presentation  
**Date:** July 29, 2026  
**Scope:** Entergy Louisiana generation, transmission, data-center load, solar, gas, nuclear, and rate-recovery items relevant to the 337.NEWS energy investigation.

## Why this source matters

Entergy's investor presentation places large-load/data-center growth, generation additions, transmission investment, renewable subscriptions, nuclear uprates, and regulatory recovery in the same corporate planning framework. This is useful context for evaluating Louisiana's changing load requirements and the different technologies being proposed to meet them. It does **not** establish any relationship between Entergy, Persimmon, Azimuth, BECi, or 1803 beyond relationships independently documented elsewhere.

## System-level context

As of December 31, 2025, Entergy described itself as a vertically integrated utility with five operating companies in four states, 3.1 million retail customers, 24,621 MW of owned/leased generating assets, 16,128 circuit miles of interconnected high-voltage transmission, and 107,838 circuit miles of distribution.

Entergy reported 2025 energy sources, including owned and purchased resources, as approximately:
- 38% combined-cycle/combustion turbine
- 28% nuclear
- 18% purchases
- 8% legacy gas/oil
- 5% coal
- 3% renewables

The presentation notes that some renewable and nuclear energy is procured/produced for customers under special tariffs, contracts, or renewable-program subscriptions.

## Large-load / data-center risk and planning

Entergy's forward-looking disclosures specifically identify:
- rapidly growing electricity demand from large-scale data centers and other large customers;
- the possibility that contracted or expected load growth may not materialize;
- generation/transmission proceedings and political opposition involving facilities designed to serve large-scale data centers;
- policy changes concerning data-center energy use, efficiency standards, sources of power, and new projects serving large loads.

This establishes that large data centers are material to Entergy's generation, transmission, regulatory, and capital-planning outlook.

## Meta / Evest — Docket U-37882

Entergy Louisiana describes proposed generation and transmission resources supporting load growth including a new north-Louisiana data center.

The presentation identifies:
- seven CCCT units totaling more than 5,200 MW — approximately $12.9 billion;
- transmission and related facilities — approximately $1.5 billion;
- BESS — approximately $0.7 billion plus TBD;
- an electric-service agreement permitting subscription to as much as an additional 2,500 MW of renewable resources under E-LA's existing green tariff;
- an agreement concerning funding of planned and potential nuclear uprates.

**Investigative significance:** This is primary-source evidence that Entergy's large-data-center strategy can combine gas generation, transmission, batteries, renewable subscriptions, and nuclear uprates. It should not be characterized as a simple solar-versus-nuclear strategy.

## Westlake CCCT — Docket U-37853

Entergy Louisiana proposes:
- 754 MW CCCT;
- Westlake, Louisiana;
- estimated cost about $2.1 billion including transmission interconnection and related costs;
- targeted in-service date 2030;
- proposed recovery through ACM / FRP or another base-recovery mechanism and TRM.

**Investigative significance:** This is a major proposed firm-generation investment in Southwest Louisiana and should be included in comparisons of regional solar, gas, nuclear, transmission, and large-load policy.

## Entergy Louisiana ratemaking context

The presentation reports E-LA:
- approximately 1.115 million customers;
- authorized ROE range 9.3%–10.1%;
- FRP with generation, transmission, and distribution riders;
- $16.7B base rate base plus identified TRM, DRM, and RPCR additions.

The 2026 annual FRP filing (U-37950) reports $17B rate base plus $0.8B of transmission, distribution, and generation plant closings through August 31, 2026, with a proposed total FRP change of $149M.

## Reporting cautions

1. Entergy's investor presentation contains forward-looking statements; projected costs, dates, load, approvals, and construction can change.
2. A project appearing in Entergy's portfolio does not establish that it serves Persimmon, Azimuth, BECi, or 1803.
3. Data-center-driven investment in north Louisiana is a documented example of utility planning, not proof of an analogous arrangement in Moss Bluff.
4. Generation ownership, wholesale procurement, transmission ownership/control, certificated retail service, and physical delivery are separate questions.
5. Entergy's simultaneous investment in renewables, gas, storage, transmission, and nuclear should be preserved when characterizing its strategy.
