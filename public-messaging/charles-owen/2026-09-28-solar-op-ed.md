# Charles Owen Op-Ed — “Warning: Don’t Trade the Farm for a Fad”

**Classification:** PRIMARY PUBLIC STATEMENT / externally published commentary  
**Author:** Rep. Charles “Chuck” Owen  
**Published:** September 28, 2026  
**Publisher:** The Hayride  
**Source:** https://thehayride.com/2026/09/owen-warning-dont-trade-the-farm-for-a-fad/

> This repository does not reproduce the full copyrighted article. The source is linked above and the material below is a reporter's evidence summary with limited excerpts.

## Core positions documented in the op-ed

Owen frames the utility-scale solar dispute as a conflict between legitimate property interests: a landowner's right to lease property and neighboring landowners' interests in drainage, livestock, rural character, and property use.

He argues that large solar installations should be discussed as **industrial generating facilities**, rather than treated rhetorically as conventional agriculture.

Owen also connects solar leasing to weakness in traditional rural land markets, particularly timber. He argues that landowners may accept long-term solar leases because traditional uses no longer provide adequate returns.

His proposed land-use response includes setbacks, notice requirements, drainage rules, decommissioning bonds, and agricultural-land screening.

## Generation / grid position

The op-ed does **not** argue that solar produces no useful electricity. Owen explicitly states that solar can add power and can have a place as augmentation.

His central grid argument is that weather-dependent solar should not be treated as a substitute for firm/dispatchable generation. He expressly identifies **natural gas and nuclear** as generation that operates more consistently and argues that grid reliability requires firm backup, storage, and transmission when intermittent resources are added.

Limited excerpt:

> “Use solar where it helps.”

Owen's stated distinction is therefore not simply “solar bad / nuclear good.” His published position combines:

- property rights;
- rural land-use protection;
- skepticism toward industrial-scale solar siting;
- concern about intermittency and capacity;
- support for maintaining firm generation, explicitly including nuclear and natural gas;
- continued acceptance of solar where he believes its scale and function are appropriate.

## Geographic relevance

The op-ed expressly refers to solar-placement disputes reaching **Calcasieu** and says discussions are occurring in western Louisiana.

Owen represents House District 30, including portions of Beauregard and Vernon parishes. The broader investigation separately examines BECi/1803 because BECi is headquartered in DeRidder and participates in 1803 Electric Cooperative.

The op-ed itself does **not** establish that BECi, 1803, Entergy, Persimmon/Orion, or another energy entity influenced Owen's position.

## Investigative significance

This is a significant dated marker in the investigation because Owen publicly articulates essentially an **energy-mix and land-use doctrine**:

1. Preserve rural property rights on both sides of a project boundary.
2. Treat utility-scale solar as industrial development for siting purposes.
3. Strengthen traditional agricultural/timber economics.
4. Use solar as supplemental generation where appropriate.
5. Retain firm generation and explicitly include nuclear in that mix.

This gives the investigation a concrete basis for comparing Owen's public position with Geymann's legislative activity and statements without assuming the two are acting in concert.
